# MFBC background (for outreach drafting)

This file is reusable context the email-drafting feature uses to ground
outreach drafts in real facts about the club. It is public information and
safe to commit — unlike the real outreach history, nothing here is private.

Edit this file (or the matching section in the app) whenever these facts
change; the drafting tool reads it fresh each time.

## What MFBC is

The Madison Fashion Business Collective (MFBC) is a UW-Madison student
organization founded in 2025 by co-founders/co-presidents Gaby Goldberg,
Tess Parker, and Sam Waxer. It connects UW-Madison students with the
fashion, business, and media industries beyond campus.

MFBC is distinct from UW's other fashion org, The Vault: Business Behind
Fashion — MFBC focuses specifically on media strategy and direct engagement
with industry professionals (guest speakers, networking events, and
presentations spotlighting fashion entrepreneurs and industry news), rather
than general fashion-business education.

Members work on one of three teams: marketing, social media, and creative
partnerships. Membership is open to UW-Madison students with at least a
3.0 GPA.

## The flagship event: the fall convention

MFBC's primary annual event is a fall convention featuring at least eight
notable influencers and business leaders giving talks, followed by
small-group coffee chats with attendees. This is the event outreach to
speakers and sponsors is usually in service of.

Past/aspirational speaker references include entrepreneur Danielle
Bernstein (founder of WeWoreWhat) and Max Siegelman (founder of Siegelman
Stable), though the specific lineup for any given semester should come from
current planning, not be assumed from this file.

## How this should be used in drafts

When drafting outreach, a member or speaker should understand *why MFBC is
reaching out to them specifically* — e.g., visibility to a curated audience
of fashion/business-minded UW-Madison students, a speaking slot at the fall
convention in front of students who will become industry professionals,
a flea-market vendor/pop-up opportunity, or a social-media content
collaboration. The draft should connect the brand's own identity to one of
these real MFBC offerings, not a generic "we'd love to partner" line — but
it must rely only on facts given here or by the member, never invented
specifics about the brand or about MFBC's current plans (e.g. don't state
a convention date, attendee count, or "we worked with you before" unless
that was actually provided).
